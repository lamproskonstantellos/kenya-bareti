BARETI Module 1: Sustainable Energy Planning Training

10 to 11 November 2026, Ibis Hotel, Nairobi, Kenya

Two days on how to plan a power system, from one home to the whole country. This page is a short guide: what we did, what we learned and what the room answered.

Trainers: Tim Lorenz and Lampros Konstantellos, Technical University of Munich (TUM). Organised by bbw international and AHK Eastern Africa.

The two days in numbers

  • 38players in Quiz 1 on Day 1, the highest count of the two days. One player is one phone.
  • 47notes on the Miro board in the activity One word per table.
  • 9plans for Kenya's 2035 mix on the class board of the lab.
  • 7Kahoot games on the phones: four quizzes and three polls.

Four sessions, four questions

Each session picks up the question that the one before leaves open.

  • Day 1, Lampros Konstantellos

    Fundamentals and Methods of Integrated Energy Planning

    What should Kenya build by 2035, and why?

    What we did
    The planning cycle, the 2030 peak in pairs, the cost of a kWh, and Kenya's 2035 mix in the lab.
    What we learned
    Least cost for one future is not robust for many: test it, and count the regret.

    The sessionSlides (PDF)

  • Day 1, Tim Lorenz

    Integration of Renewable Energy

    How do wind and solar fit into the grid?

    What we did
    Six properties of wind and photovoltaics (PV), four levers, and the Investment Simulation in three phases.
    What we learned
    Siting and technology are decisions about the system, not only about one project.

    The sessionSlides (PDF)

  • Day 2, Tim Lorenz

    Energy Modelling at Household Level

    What can one home do with PV and a battery?

    What we did
    A demand profile, a guess for PV and battery, a simulation and the cost optimum in the household energy lab.
    What we learned
    The difference between the guess and the optimum is the lesson of the morning.

    The sessionSlides (PDF)

  • Day 2, Lampros Konstantellos

    Energy Modelling at National Scale

    How does the whole grid run, hour by hour?

    What we did
    Kenya next to five countries, how a model sees the system, and three table activities on Miro.
    What we learned
    Flexibility has four sources: hydro, storage, demand response and trade.

    The sessionSlides (PDF)

Who was in the room

Most participants were engineers from utilities and private companies. They wanted practice: 23 of 30 chose hands-on tools and models or methods they can use at work.

IPP: independent power producer. EPC: engineering, procurement and construction. NGO: non-governmental organisation. All seven questions

What the room knew: the four quizzes

Quiz 1 and Quiz 3 were warm-ups before the teaching. Quiz 2 and Quiz 4 came at the end and check the methods of the day. A question ran for 20 or 30 seconds, so a low share reflects time pressure as well as knowledge.

Power at home

The participants live with frequent power cuts, and the interest in backup is clear. The group is small and does not represent Kenyan households.
